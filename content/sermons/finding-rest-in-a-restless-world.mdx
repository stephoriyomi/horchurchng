## Matthew 11:28–30

> "Come to me, all who are weary and burdened, and I will give you rest. Take my yoke upon you and learn from me, for I am gentle and humble in heart, and you will find rest for your souls. For my yoke is easy and my burden is light."

---

We live in a world that has redefined productivity as virtue and rest as laziness.

The average professional in Lagos is juggling at least two jobs, a side hustle, family obligations, and the expectation to *always be available*. Rest feels earned at best and irresponsible at worst.

And somewhere in that exhaustion, we started to believe that God operates the same way. That grace is conditional. That you have to show up right, pray right, believe right — or you don't qualify.

### What "Rest" Actually Means

The Greek word Jesus uses here is *anapausis* — it means more than sleep. It means relief from labour. Peace of mind. Settledness of soul.

Jesus isn't promising a nap. He's promising **shalom** — the Hebrew concept of wholeness, of everything being as it should be. Not the absence of difficulty, but peace *in* the middle of it.

### The Invitation Is for the Weary

Notice who the invitation is *for*: those who are weary and burdened. Not the put-together. Not the spiritual elite. Not those who have it all figured out.

The invitation is specifically for people who are *tired*.

Tired of performing. Tired of pretending. Tired of carrying weight they were never meant to carry.

If that's you today — this message is for you.

### Taking His Yoke

A yoke was a farming tool — a wooden frame that connected two animals to share the burden of work. When Jesus says "take my yoke," he's inviting us into a different way of working. Not *no* work — but work done differently, alongside someone who knows the way.

**The difference between your yoke and his:**

- Your yoke demands performance. His yoke starts with grace.
- Your yoke says "earn it." His yoke says "receive it."
- Your yoke gets heavier with time. His gets lighter as you learn.

### Practical Steps This Week

1. **Name what you're carrying.** What burden have you been dragging around that was never meant for you?
2. **Say it out loud.** There's something powerful about voicing the weight before God.
3. **Come back.** One encounter won't undo years of exhaustion — but this is a beginning.
