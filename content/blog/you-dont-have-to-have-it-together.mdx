*I've been on both sides of this.*

I was the one who showed up every Sunday, sang every song, served on every team. And I was the one who, eight years later, sat in my car in the church car park and couldn't make myself go inside.

Not because something dramatic happened. It was more like a slow erosion — the quiet accumulation of small disappointments. The way spiritual success got measured by attendance. The way asking hard questions made people uncomfortable. The way grace was preached on Sundays but withheld in the car park.

I left quietly. No announcement, no confrontation. I just stopped coming.

---

### Three Years Outside

Those three years were some of the most honest of my life.

I read widely — theology, philosophy, science. I sat with friends who had no faith and listened carefully to why they found it implausible. I asked questions I had never allowed myself to ask inside the building.

And somewhere in all of that, I didn't lose my faith. I *clarified* it.

I stopped believing in the performance version of God — the one keeping score, measuring devotion, disappointed in every slip. And I started to encounter something older and stranger: a grace I couldn't earn, couldn't lose, couldn't fully explain.

### Why I Came Back

I didn't come back because someone persuaded me.

I came back because I started to believe, slowly, that community matters — that being human in the way Jesus described is not a solo project. And because I had finally found a place that seemed to understand that rest and honesty belong in the same room as faith.

### What I Want You to Know

If you're reading this and you've never tried church, or you walked away and haven't looked back — I understand. Completely.

I'm not here to tell you to come back. I'm here to tell you that if you *do* come back, you're allowed to come back *honest*. Allowed to bring your doubt, your disappointment, your unresolved questions.

That's exactly what this place is for.

The door is open. Come as you are — not as you think you should be.
